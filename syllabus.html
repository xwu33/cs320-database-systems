Course Objectives

CS 320 introduces the fundamental principles of database systems. The objectives of this course are to provide a basic understanding of the strategies and problems associated with designing, querying, and managing database systems. The course presents the relational and ER data models and covers the basic concepts of database management systems. We will use a relational database management system and SQL. We will also discuss the emerging data model of NoSQL for non-structured data. A knowledge of programming languages (Java/C++), data structures, and the application development process is assumed.

Texts

  • Fundamentals of Database Systems, Elmasri and Navathe, 7th Edition, Addison Wesley, 2015 (2016). Required.
  • Supplemental text: Database Systems – Introduction to Databases and Data Warehouses, 2013.

Prerequisite

CS 141, CS 142, CS 220

Course Catalog Description

Constituent parts of database management (design, creation, and manipulation of databases), including the conceptual and relational data models, SQL, normalization, and security. Writing proficiency within this discipline is required for a passing grade in this course.

Topics and Chapters to Be Covered

Subject to change.

  • Relational model

Policy Regarding Make-ups and Late Assignments

  • If you miss an assignment or exam, email the instructor as soon as possible to determine the appropriate make-up action. Make arrangements before known absences when possible. Non-valid excuses result in a score of zero for the exam.
  • Students receive two individual slip days. Slip days are calendar days; if class does not meet on one of those days, email the assignment to the instructor. For group assignments, each group member loses a slip day when the work is late. Regardless of slip days, late homework is not accepted after graded assignments have been returned to the class.
  • All make-up actions must be completed before tests or assignments are graded and returned. No make-up actions will be taken after that time.

Exams, Assignments, and Writing Proficiency

The archived syllabus states that it is almost impossible to pass the course after failing both midterms. It emphasizes that professional work should be neat, readable, coherent, complete, and understandable. Writing proficiency is required to pass the course, regardless of performance on other requirements. Students who do not pass all writing assignments receive a failing grade.

For the Database Design and Implementation Project, students write a project report describing requirements collection and analysis, the miniworld, schema diagram evolution, and a comparison of initial and final requirements. Students also write papers comparing and recommending current database techniques using journal or industry articles or performance results, and addressing a current topic in databases. The first writing assignment is graded and returned by midterm.

Severe Weather Guidelines

The University of Alabama's guiding principle is the personal safety of students, faculty, and staff during severe weather. These guidelines provide additional help in responding to weather-related emergencies; they cannot anticipate every situation.

Classes generally remain in session until the National Weather Service issues safety warnings for Tuscaloosa. Commuters should follow National Weather Service advice for their area and take precautions. When a warning is issued, people in the storm's path should take immediate life-saving action.

When West Alabama is under a severe weather advisory, conditions can change rapidly. Get to a place where you can receive information from the National Weather Service and follow the instructions provided. Personal safety should guide decisions.

The archived syllabus lists these University Relations channels for campus updates:

  • Weather advisory on the UA homepage
  • UA Alerts to faculty, staff, and students
  • WVUA at 90.7 FM
  • Alabama Public Radio (WUAL) at 91.5 FM
  • WVUA-TV/WUOA-TV and wvua23.com/weather

For a tornado warning, university activities are suspended. If indoors, move immediately to the lowest level and toward the center of the building, away from windows, and remain there until the warning expires. Classes in session may resume after the warning expires at the instructor's discretion. Classes not yet begun resume 30 minutes after the warning expires if at least half of the class period remains.
